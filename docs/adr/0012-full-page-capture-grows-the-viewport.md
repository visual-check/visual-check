# A full-page capture grows the viewport to the page

Before a full-page capture, visual-check grows the viewport's height to the page's before the `wait` hook and, when a hook ran, measures the page again after it at the story's own viewport, and captures at that height. Capturing beyond the viewport without resizing it, as the capture engine first did, lost because Chromium lays out fixed and sticky elements against the viewport, so on a page taller than it they were drawn mid-page: a sticky save bar at about 800 px over a form field of a 3,505 px page, and a scroll-locked page with an open drawer drew the drawer over the top 851 px, a blank band and then the end of the page, a raster that changed on every capture ([#164](https://github.com/visual-check/visual-check/issues/164)). Growing until the height stops changing, as some harnesses do, lost because a page with `min-height: 100vh` under a header grows by the header each time. A cap on that loop would still stretch every `100vh` section, so it buys nothing over one growth. Leaving the growth opt-in lost because every project with a sticky footer or a drawer would get mid-page snapshots until it found the option. Measuring only before the hook lost because a hook that waits for content changes the page's height after the measure: a page that got shorter was captured with a blank band at its bottom, one that fitted its viewport was captured beyond it with fixed and sticky elements mid-page, and which a run got depended on when the data landed ([#174](https://github.com/visual-check/visual-check/issues/174)).

## Consequences

- A snapshot shows the page as a screen as tall as the page shows it, with fixed and sticky elements at the page's bottom, as harnesses that resize the viewport before capturing show it.
- `100vh` becomes the page's height, as it was last measured: once the story's images and fonts were ready, or after its `wait` hook when one ran, so a full-screen section grows with the page. `growViewport: false` keeps the viewport's height for a story that needs it.
- The viewport grows after the first check of images and fonts and before the `wait` hook, so the hook runs at the grown size. When it grew, two frames pass before anything goes on, so the page's reaction to the new size, such as an `IntersectionObserver` scroll-spy marking the last section, is done before the capture rather than in the second after it ([#169](https://github.com/visual-check/visual-check/issues/169)). Then images and fonts are checked again, since a taller viewport can show lazy images.
- When the story's `wait` hook ran, the page is measured again after the hook and its check of images and fonts, before the end state: the story's own viewport is set again, then grows to the page's height when the page is taller. A page the hook made shorter shrinks back, so its snapshot has no blank band, and one it made taller grows, so its fixed and sticky elements sit at its bottom. Measuring at the story's own viewport, not the grown one, keeps a `min-height: 100vh` page from growing again. Two frames pass whether it grew or not, since setting the viewport fires `resize` too, then images and fonts are checked again. It happens once, with no loop, and not for a story without a hook that ran. Setting the viewport again leaves touch emulation as it is, so the page keeps its hover.
- Content that grows with the viewport isn't chased: what lies below the grown viewport is captured beyond it, as before.
- Each tall story's snapshot changed once, so its baseline needs `update` after the upgrade. A tall full-page story whose `wait` hook changes the page's height may change height once more with [#174](https://github.com/visual-check/visual-check/issues/174), so its baseline needs `update` again.
- A page over 32,767 device pixels tall keeps its viewport, including one that got that tall during its `wait` hook: it stays at the story's viewport, and its capture is `too-tall` as before.
